We touched on this subject when we discussed the philosophy of mind. It is a widely debated subject both in scientific and philosophical circles.



What is the difference between objective and subjective? If a person perceives something that is the same as how all the other people perceive, it is objective. If it is different for different people, it is subjective. We assume that the colour of an object is objective whereas a sense of beauty is subjective. Before we dive deeper, I need to clear some confusion around the word “relative”. While “relative” is used interchangeably with “subjective”, it could also mean something different. When we describe an object we observe in terms of another object, then relative is the better word. For example,

*This blue is darker than the other blue.*

*The first bedroom is bigger than the second bedroom.*



Science deals with the concept of subjectivity using the term “relativity”. For a long time, there were very few phenomena in the physical world that were treated as relative, the important one being velocity. If you are standing on a platform and a train is coming at 10kmph, you feel that it is 10kmph fast. If someone else is in a train that travels at 10kmph towards the other train, then that person feels that the opposite train is travelling at 20kmph. So velocity is relative.



Other concepts like space and time were considered absolute. If you measure the length of an object, it is assumed that it would be the same as someone else measuring the length of the same object. We expect that the same time is measured in different watches unless there is a problem in the watch. As we learned in the chapter on classical physics, both of these came into question when Einstein published his theory of relativity. We found that the length of an object measured by one person could be different to the length of the same object measured by another person who is travelling fast relative to the first person. The difference will be insignificant unless the person is travelling to the speed of light. That is why we don’t notice this difference.



Same with time. The time measurements in the watches of the two people could be different if one person is moving with respect to the other person. This also happens when one of the two is on a planet of huge mass as gravity bends spacetime.



What is the implication? Space and Time are also relative. If that is the case, what is absolute? The current consensus is that spacetime is absolute, not space or time individually. We just move(if we can use this word when we talk about spacetime instead of just space) from a point to point in spacetime. The spacetime graph is dynamic, not static as we thought as it can be bent and stretched by mass. But it can be treated as objective, at least as far as we know.



Physicists hate it when people say, “Everything is relative. Even Einstein agrees.” This is because spacetime is still objective according to relativity and it is wrong to miss this completely.

An interesting topic on this subject is the Mach principle. He said that the inertia of an object is measured against the mass in the rest of the universe. If the universe doesn’t have any other mass and just one object, this object will not feel inertia. Imagine a bucket half filled with water. If it is rotated, you will see the water around the sides of the bucket go up because of inertia. Mach’s theory is that this wouldn’t happen in an empty universe. It is a theory that has neither been proven or disproven. I will email you all when we have an empty universe with just a bucket full of water. But… Never mind.



Now, let us move on to the philosophical side. We already discussed the subjectivity problem with mind. We assume that our direct experience of the external world is objective though we agree that the second hand effects like our feelings towards what we see in the external world is subjective. A painting looks the same to everyone though some may find it beautiful while the others find it revolting. A roller-coaster ride is fun for some, vomit-inducing for others. What if our assumption is wrong and the way different people experience these things are actually different? Remember the inverted qualia problem? What if the other person’s blue is different from your blue?

Then there are much more touchy subjects like morals. What is considered “good” by one person may be considered “bad” by another.



Lastly, there is the topic of God or the Absolute that we discussed in the last chapter. There are philosophies which claim that all our experiences are subjective and limited while the Absolute is objective and infinite.



There are numerous things in our day-to-day life where the objectivity-subjectivity problem comes up though we never address it directly. Philosophers, on the other hand, like a good fight over it.
