After breezing through the different philosophies, their problems and paradoxes, where do we stand now? Did we even get close to answering the *What Is?* question. No we didn't. Every puzzle we solve leads us to more puzzles. Every assumption that we make that explains one property of our experience seems to struggle with the others. We just have a bunch of potential solutions which have their own problems.



Here is the situation described from a bird's eye view. Our mind is trying to explain the world and the mind itself. Our mind is capable of conceiving numerous, potentially infinite possibilities that could explain these.

* We have empirical knowledge about the world we perceive which along with our rational knowledge helps us discount some possibilities that are logically inconsistent as impossible. And we are still left with infinite potential explanations.
* Empirical knowledge of the mind is trickier: the mind is the very tool we use to observe anything, so studying it isn't like pointing a telescope at a planet.



And we are given the difficult task of finding a philosophy among the infinite potential possibilities. Let’s say we pick one and build an informal philosophical system that is consistent. It is never completely proven. Based on the Münchhausen trilemma, it will have at least one of the following:

1) Brute statements taken to be facts.
2) Self-referential cycles.
3) Acceptance of infinite regressions



The problem with brute statements - They always lead to more questions. You want to use God as an entity to explain everything? Where does God come from? You think today's best physics - the Standard Model - is the final word? It still can't say why we have exactly these particles with exactly these masses and charges. 

You could say that these are just brute statements that have to be taken as facts. Or you take one of the other two options of the Trilemma - Either use self-referential cycles or infinite regressions. For example, Some say God just exists without needing a cause (a brute fact); some say God is self-existent (a self reference); a few, like the mathematician Cantor, pictured God as a kind of endless infinity (Infinite regress).



The problem with self-references and infinite regressions is that they are outright scary and lead to numerous paradoxes which stump us. But as we have seen, both the physical world and the mind could potentially have infinities and self-loops.



The mind alone has problems of its own. The **I**, the sense of self feels lasting, unitary and unchanging. But the physical world keeps changing and matter can be broken down into smaller and smaller pieces until we hit the minute fundamental particles, which also behave like waves. That, along with the *Hard Problem of Consciousness* makes it hard to explain the mind in terms of physical entities. But if we decide to use non-physical entities to explain the mind, we run into other problems.

* If the physical brain is not what gives rise to the mental experience, and there is some entity external to the physical world, how do you know who has a conscious experience? All humans? Only some humans? Some animals too? There is no way to know because we don't know anything about this external entity.
* How does this non-physical entity interact with the physical world that seems to follow laws? If a non-physical entity can influence this physical world, surely we would have noticed events which don't agree with the physical laws?

Irrespective of the philosophy, there is also the question of free-will. Is it real or illusion? If it is real, where does it come from? 



Considering all this, what are our options?

#### Don’t know. Don’t care.

Do you even need a philosophy that understands the nature of reality? Billions of people have lived and died without needing one. So why bother? Just get on with your life without thinking too much. Let’s partyyyyyy!!!

#### I don't know - but I will find the truth

Admit ignorance, then commit to the search. Keep asking why, keep testing, keep refining. There is no guarantee the search ends - this book has shown you why it might not - but the search itself is worthwhile. Every paradox you untangle and every assumption you expose is real progress, even if the final answer stays out of reach. The joy is in the hunting, not just the catching.

#### Compromise and pick a complete philosophy with brute statements

Build yourself one or pick an existing philosophical system that has a lot of brute statements which are taken as fundamental truths. Remember that all of these have some uncomfortable moral implications that you have to grapple with.

* For example, you can pick physicalism and brush away the problem of qualia using brute statements. But how do you handle the identity problem? You can worry about it when human cloning and teleportation really becomes a thing.
* If you pick dualism, you have to face the uncomfortable question of which animals, insects or microorganisms around you really have the mental substance/property. Or you could just not care.
* If you pick idealism, you still face the same question as dualism. Which animals, insects or microbes are having a conscious experience and aren’t just zombies in the dream.



Just remember…..

#### Embrace the infinities and self-references

Try to embrace the self-references and infinites. Pick a philosophy that uses infinite regressions and self-references instead of relying on brute statements. Swim in the paradoxes of self-references. Chase those infinities. Maybe take the mystic path and see if they were right. Maybe you will really understand the absolute infinity with your mystic knowledge.


Going back to what I said earlier, the mind is trying to explain reality that includes the world and the mind itself. There are numerous (potentially infinite?) possibilities that the mind could think of, to explain reality, most of which can be categorised into the four options I have mentioned above. The situation may look overwhelming. But remember that its your own mind that conceived all of these possibilities. That is how powerful your mind is. Of the four options I have listed above, no one option is better than any other option. Go for what you feel works for you. You can always change it later if it doesn’t work. That’s the best part about having so many choices.
